# Primary Health Care (PHC) Distribution and Coverage Analysis Report

## Project Title
Primary Health Care (PHC) Distribution and Coverage Analysis – Nigeria

## Project Objectives
- To assess the distribution of Primary Health Care facilities across wards in Nigeria.
- To evaluate population-to-PHC ratios and identify underserved wards.
- To examine facility functionality, health workforce availability, and immunization performance.
- To highlight priority areas requiring intervention and provide actionable recommendations for improving PHC access and service delivery.

## Dataset Used
Integrated dataset combining:
- Ward population estimates
- Settlement distribution
- PHC facility locations and functionality status
- Health workforce data (doctors, nurses/midwives, health attendants, skilled birth attendants)
- Immunization coverage indicators at LGA level

**Scope:** 5,692 wards analysed nationwide.

## Key Questions Explored
1. How are PHCs distributed across wards in Nigeria?
2. What is the population pressure on existing PHC facilities?
3. Which wards have inadequate PHC coverage relative to population and settlements?
4. What is the functionality status of existing PHC facilities?
5. How severe are health workforce shortages across facilities?
6. Which areas show poor immunization performance, and how does this relate to PHC coverage?
7. Which wards should be prioritised for new PHCs, staffing, or infrastructure investment?

## Process
1. **Data Integration**  
   Merged ward-level population, settlement, PHC facility, staffing, and immunization datasets into a unified analysis workbook.

2. **Coverage & Need Assessment**  
   Calculated population-to-PHC ratios and generated priority classifications and PHC Need Flags.

3. **Functionality & Workforce Analysis**  
   Reviewed facility functionality status and staffing levels across key healthcare cadres.

4. **Gap Identification & Prioritisation**  
   Ranked wards by service pressure and classified them into Critical, High, and Medium priority categories.

5. **Recommendation Development**  
   Formulated short-term, medium-term, and long-term intervention strategies.

**Highest Population-to-PHC Pressure (Top examples)**
- Kakau (Chikun, Kaduna) – 605,968 people per PHC
- Namtari (Yola South, Adamawa) – 559,491
- Rigasa (Igabi, Kaduna) – 406,303
- Galadima (Gusau, Zamfara) – 302,113
- Kabusa (FCT) – 297,505

**Other Critical Insights**
- Many facilities are only partially functional due to staffing and infrastructure gaps.
- Significant shortages of doctors, nurses/midwives, and skilled birth attendants.
- Poor immunization coverage frequently correlates with inadequate PHC presence and weak outreach.

## Recommendations

**Short-Term**
- Deploy additional medical officers, nurses/midwives, CHEWs, and skilled birth attendants.
- Upgrade partially functional facilities (equipment, drugs, infrastructure, utilities).
- Expand mobile clinics and immunization outreach in remote settlements.

**Medium-Term**
- Construct new PHCs in wards flagged as “HIGH NEED – Add PHC”.
- Establish population-based planning standards (facility-to-population and geographic access benchmarks).
- Improve data quality and reporting systems.

**Long-Term**
- Develop a national GIS-based PHC optimisation framework.
- Integrate immunization performance data into PHC planning and investment decisions.

## Conclusion
While Primary Health Care infrastructure exists across many wards in Nigeria, substantial disparities remain in access, staffing, and service capacity. Of the 5,692 wards assessed, **1,320 are classified as high priority** and **3,535 wards require additional PHCs**. Several wards face extreme population pressure per facility.

Closing these gaps through targeted facility expansion, workforce strengthening, and infrastructure rehabilitation will significantly improve healthcare access, immunization outcomes, maternal health, and overall community well-being.
